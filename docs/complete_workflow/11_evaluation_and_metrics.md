The following metrics represent the verified, measured test results serialized in `results/reports/final_metrics.json`:

| Metric Name | Mathematical Formula | Measured LANL Value | Operational Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | `(TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)` | **84.08%** (243 / 289) | Overall proportion of correct classifications |

## 2. Scientific Analysis: The Base-Rate Fallacy and Accuracy in Cybersecurity

A critical concept for project defense and technical interviews is understanding why **high accuracy alone is completely meaningless in cybersecurity machine learning**.

### The Accuracy Paradox
Suppose an enterprise network processes 1,000,000 log events per day, of which only 100 are malicious attacks (a positive class prevalence of 0.01%):
- A trivial "dummy classifier" that predicts every single event is benign achieves **99.99% accuracy**.
- However, its Recall is **0.00%**, meaning it allows 100% of cyber breaches to succeed completely undetected!
- Conversely, a model with 95% accuracy in this environment would generate 50,000 false alarms per day, completely overwhelming the SOC team (alert fatigue).

### Why AI Investigator Prioritizes Specificity and Precision
In the AI Investigator platform:
1. **Specificity is prioritized at 93.78%**, maintaining a low false positive rate (6.22%). This ensures that SOC analysts are not overwhelmed with spurious alerts.
2. **Sub-threshold attacks are captured by the multi-agent mesh:** Even when the ML model's probability falls below the alerting threshold (resulting in an ML false negative), deterministic agent rules (such as R15 for LOLBins or R2 for brute-force bursts) independently detect the threat.
